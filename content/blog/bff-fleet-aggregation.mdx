A single learner screen in Microlearning — progress, profile, entitlements, assessment state,
notifications — needs data that lives behind roughly eight separate internal services. The obvious
shape is "the frontend calls what it needs." The obvious shape is also wrong the moment you look at
what it costs.

## What fan-out from the browser actually buys you

Nothing, and it charges for the privilege. Eight services means eight sets of credentials that would
have to live somewhere the browser can reach — which means in the browser. It means eight CORS
configurations, eight token-refresh paths, eight places a client-side bug can leak or mishandle a
credential. And it means the public internet gets a direct line to eight internal surfaces instead of
one, because "the frontend talks to what it needs" is also "an attacker talks to what the frontend
could reach."

## The BFF is a trust boundary, not a proxy

A Backend-for-Frontend that just relays requests one-for-one doesn't solve this — it's the same
fan-out with an extra hop. The version that earns its keep is a stateless aggregator: one request in
from the browser, one resolved identity threaded through every downstream call, one composed
response out. Every downstream credential lives in the BFF's environment and never crosses into
client-side code. The browser has exactly one origin to trust.

```text
Learner PWA ──▶ Go BFF (auth, aggregation) ──▶ [progress · profile · entitlements ·
                                                  assessments · identity · notifications · …]
```

## Why stateless matters as much as the aggregation

The BFF holds no database of its own. It resolves who the caller is, fans the request out, composes
what comes back, and forgets everything the moment the response is sent. That keeps the
security-sensitive component small enough to actually reason about, and it makes horizontal scaling a
non-event — there's no state to shard or replicate, just more identical processes doing the same
stateless work.

## Identity resolved once, not eight times

The alternative to a shared identity-resolution step is each downstream call re-deriving "who is this
and what should they see" from a raw token, independently, eight times, with eight chances to get it
subtly wrong. Resolving locale, contract, and onboarding state once per request and threading that
resolved context downstream means every service gets a consistent view of the caller instead of
re-implementing the same derivation.

## Where this came from

This is Microlearning's actual shape: a Go BFF in front of roughly eight internal services, serving
50,000+ learners, with every downstream credential kept server-side. The interesting engineering
wasn't any individual screen — it was accepting that the aggregation and the trust boundary are the
same component, and building that component to be boring, stateless, and easy to scale rather than
clever.
